01 — Brand Idea

A cycle that keeps itself going.

The product is a deterministic AI engine that carries a lead from first contact to a closed deal on a fixed script (a "Business DNA"), never promising anything the script doesn't cover. The product's own funnel is built the same way and sells the product to itself — a closed, self-sustaining loop.

A flywheel is a part you spin up once; after that it holds its own rotation and gives back energy evenly, turn after turn, with no jolts. This isn't a metaphor picked for looks — the engineering principle of a flywheel is a literal description of how the product works.

The core brand idea. A flywheel doesn't need to be pushed on every turn — stored momentum does the work. Flywheel the product works the same way: a script, once set up, keeps carrying a lead toward the deal on its own, with no manual push at every step.
02 — Name

Borrowed, not coined.

Flywheel isn't a coined word — it's a term borrowed at once from engineering and from B2B marketing: the "flywheel" growth model is already familiar to the audience (HubSpot built its own methodology on it), so the meaning lands instantly, no explanation needed. The word reads and sounds the same everywhere the launch market speaks English.

One caveat. "Flywheel" is in active use across software and martech: Flywheel Digital, a registered Flywheel Software, Inc. trademark, the hosting provider getflywheel.com, and others. A bare word mark will be hard to protect outright — registered as a combined mark (logo + name), not the word alone. Full clearance findings live in the companion rebrand document.
03 — The Emblem

The Wheel.

Drawn not as a static, symmetrical circle but as a shape caught in motion — the wheel is never drawn at rest. Five spokes, not three or four: three in a ring reads instantly as a well-known automotive badge, a collision deliberately designed away from. Five keeps the silhouette unmistakably a wheel — cart wheel, ship's wheel, gear — at every size.

05 — Brand Voice

Short, confident, no superlatives.

The product doesn't "disrupt the market" — it keeps the rotation going. The flywheel metaphor belongs in headlines and on the "About" page; product copy and outreach to leads default to specifics — savings figures, response times, compliance citations.

On-brand

"The AI only rewrites the wording of a script you approve."

"$649 average cost per lead in legal services."

Specific, verifiable, no adjectives doing the work a fact could do instead.

Off-brand

"Revolutionary AI that transforms your intake forever."

"The smartest assistant your firm will ever meet."

Superlatives and hype — the kind of language a deterministic product doesn't need to reach for.

06 — Brand Legend

Civilisational, not mythological.

Before the wheel, everything moved only because someone pushed it, dragged it, carried it — every time, from the start. Then someone noticed a stone roll downhill on its own, needing no second push, and understood: motion doesn't have to be spent all over again each time. It can be started once and kept going.

So the wheel arrived — not one invention among many, but the one that made the rest possible. The cart, the potter's wheel, the mill, and much later the flywheel of the steam engine, holding the turn between each stroke of power so that no force was wasted, only stored and returned, evenly. Each of those was its own era's wheel — the same idea, turned toward whatever that age needed moving.

We're not finishing that story. We're giving the wheel a new era to turn in. Flywheel takes the same principle — motion, once started, keeps itself going — and points it at business: the path from first contact to closed deal turns on its own, gaining momentum with every cycle, instead of being pushed by hand at every step.

The first wheel started motion. This one starts an era.

The legend's register is historical, not religious, and grounded in a real engineering fact — the steam-engine flywheel is history, not metaphor. It belongs on an "About" page and the opening slide of a deck. It does not belong in product copy or lead-facing sales text — see Wave 1 application below.

09 — Scaling to New Verticals & Markets

Tuned, not rebuilt.

The brand is built to move with the GTM roadmap — Wave 2 (financial and insurance advisors), then new geographies — without redesigning the system each time.

Dark Mode — Product

Graphite background, bronze line work. Used for the product itself and SaaS contexts generally — where reading as software matters more than reading as an established practice.

Light Mode — Conservative Verticals

Bronze on warm white. Used for lawyers, financial advisors, and other professional verticals where the brand needs to read as an established practice, not a startup demo. The logo and legend work in both without changes.

Culture-Neutral Emblem

The wheel carries no specific mythology or religious association — unlike an earlier concept built around the Ouroboros, set aside for exactly this reason — so no audience is narrowed by the imagery as the brand expands into new niches and countries.

Legend, Rewritten Per Market

Authored in English for the launch market first. When the brand moves into a new language market, the legend is rewritten to carry the same meaning there — not translated word for word.
