How Flywheel Is Different

Most AI intake tools run on a prompt. Flywheel runs on a script.

Prompts can be argued with, jailbroken, or drift over a long conversation — for a law firm, that's not a UX risk, it's a bar-complaint risk.

A chatbot on a prompt

  • The AI decides what to say, guided by instructions
  • Can be talked into estimating a case or promising an outcome
  • Compliance depends on the prompt holding up under pressure
  • Disclosure is whatever the prompt remembers to say

Flywheel

  • The AI only rewrites the wording of a script you approve
  • Has no path to invent legal analysis or promise a result
  • Compliance is a hard limit in the message pipeline, not a prompt
  • Identifies itself as AI from the first message, every time

FAQ

What firms ask before they start

Is this actually compliant with my state's bar rules?

Flywheel is built to support the disclosure requirements already in effect in states like California (SB 243) and New York (Article 47) — the AI identifies itself clearly, includes a "not legal advice" notice, and always offers a path to a human. Bar rules vary by state, so we'd still recommend a quick read of your own state's guidance before launch — we're not your compliance counsel.

Can the AI give legal advice by accident?

No — that's the point. The AI only rewrites wording inside the script you approve. It has no path to invent legal analysis, estimate outcomes, or promise results, because that capability isn't built into the message pipeline at all.

How long does setup take?

About 20 minutes for a single-attorney practice. No developer needed.

What if I want more than one attorney on the account?

Multi-attorney support is on our roadmap — reach out and we'll let you know when it's ready for your firm.
